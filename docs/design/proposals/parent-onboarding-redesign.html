Parent app · onboarding · redesign

Five steps, asked once

Today a family joins through two separate forms that do not know each other exists, and the second one runs again for every child. A parent with two children fills in 41 boxes, 14 of them twice. They read four phone-heights of legal prose, meet the health declaration once per child with no sense of where they are, and agree to the club's regulations somewhere in the middle. This is the arrangement that replaces it: one wizard, five named steps, and every fact asked exactly once at the level it belongs to.

The spine

One flow, and the end never moves

The five processes stop being five gates that each reappear on their own and become one wizard with a visible sequence. The count of steps is always five, whatever the size of the family — a family with one child and a family with four both see שלב 3 מתוך 5. Only the counter inside step 4 knows how many children there are.

Before the count there is only the link and a sign-in. The club posts one studio-level URL into its WhatsApp groups, so it has no idea who is opening it — no name, no children, nothing pre-filled. The first screen shows the studio's name and asks the parent to sign in, and that is all it does.

Step 3 is where the family is created. There is no separate join form before the wizard — that merge is the single largest change in this document, and the section below explains why.

Why the reading comes first

Steps 1 and 2 are the only two steps that ask a parent to agree to something rather than tell us something. Putting them together at the front means a family who walks away has typed nothing, and a family who continues never meets a legal document again. It also removes the specific complaint about the club's regulations — they no longer arrive in the middle of a sequence a parent is already tired of.

Two shapes, one wizard

An adult student is their own guardian — and in this data model that is not a special case bolted on, it is the model working as designed. student.person_id is UNIQUE, so a student is a person; the docstring says outright that §3.3 "allows an adult student who is also their own guardian." For them, step 3's row and step 4's subject are the same database row. Nothing is asked twice because there is nothing to duplicate.

What triggers it
The guardian link, not age. If the only row pointing at the student is the student's own person, they get the adult shape. A nineteen-year-old whose mother is still the guardian and the payer keeps the parent shape — because someone else really is answering for them.
What drops
Three blocks lose their meaning and are not rendered: the parents block, the pickup list, and student.grade.
What flips
Step 3's heading becomes הפרטים שלך. Step 4's questions and the declaration clause are written in the first person.
Both at once
A parent who trains and whose children train appears once as the signer in step 3 and again as the first subject in step 4's queue: אני · 1 מתוך 4, then the children. Their ת.ז. is already on file from step 3, so nothing is retyped.

Leaving and coming back

Each step writes as the parent moves off it, so closing the phone at step 4 loses nothing. Back is available throughout; returning to a recorded agreement and changing it writes a fresh record with a fresh timestamp rather than editing the old one, which is what keeps the consent ledger append-only.

The five-step spine belongs to a family arriving for the first time. When a single thing changes later — a republished privacy policy, a fourth child enrolled mid-year — the parent meets just that thing, with no wizard chrome. A progress bar over one task is noise that makes a small ask look large.

And the ordering

Form 1 renders in JoinShell, which sits outside AuthedApp and therefore outside ConsentGate. Names, birthdates and phone numbers of minors are written before a privacy policy has been agreed to. The gate nesting a few lines below argues the opposite, in writing: "the privacy policy is what permits the club to collect a medical record about a child at all, so asking for the record first and the permission afterwards has the consent doing no work." The same sentence covers a child's name.

Before step 1 — the link and the sign-in

Not a step · no counter · nothing known yet

The club posts one studio-level URL into its WhatsApp groups. It is not a per-family invitation: the club does not know who taps it, and nothing is pre-filled. So the first screen can only do two things — say which club this is, and ask the parent to sign in.

Not a second sign-in

This is the parent sign-in, with a different redirect. SignIn already takes app and returnPath, and the join route already passes /join/<token> — so the club link lands on the same screen everyone else uses and comes back to the wizard instead of the home screen. Exactly one thing differs: the studio's name above it, because a parent who tapped a link in a WhatsApp group needs to know which club this is before they sign in. Nothing else about the screen changes.

The staff app has its own ManagerSignIn face, and that stays its own — different app, different audience. A second parent face would be a third copy of the same providers and the same startUrl to keep in step, and the first time a provider is added, one of them gets forgotten.

Corrected

An earlier draft of this document opened with a welcome screen listing the children "the club already added", and a line reading 5 שלבים · כעשר דקות. Both were wrong. The first came from a canvas describing the invitation path — where a manager invites one named guardian to one named student — and the canvas spec itself said that reading "should be confirmed". It was not. The ten minutes was invented outright; the canvas carries שתי דקות for three steps and nothing measured either number. No duration appears on any screen in this design.

03

The family — created once, on one screen

You · the second parent · pickup · the students

This step is the join form. It is not a screen that follows it. The two forms shown above become one, and everything a family types happens here — which is what makes "asked once" true rather than aspirational.

The screen is organised by whose fact it is, and that single ordering rule removes every duplication at once. Your address is on your card because it is your address. Dana's ת.ז. is on Dana's row because it is Dana's. Nothing about you appears inside a card headed with a child's name, and nothing about a child appears twice.

You are never asked about yourself twice

Your name arrives from the sign-in — Google or Apple already verified it, so it is shown, not typed. Your email is the verified one on the identity, read-only, because a typed email is unverified and can be wrong.

Then one tap settles the parents question: אני: האם · האב · קרוב אחר. The club's paper form distinguishes שם האם from שם האב, and that tap is what fills the right slot. Your name and your ת.ז. are already on the card above it, so the mandatory "at least one parent" rule you asked for is satisfied by construction — it holds, and it costs nothing. The second parent's card is optional and labelled by whichever one you are not.

קרוב אחר is the one case where both slots open: a grandmother or a legal guardian is not a parent, so neither parent is on file and the club genuinely needs both names.

“I train too” is a row with one field

The checkbox exists today as selfStudent and it is kept. What changes is what it costs: your name is known, your ת.ז. is on the card above, and you have no school class. So the row asks for a group and nothing else.

And on this link it enrols rather than queues. The onboarding-link spec names itself a deliberate, scoped exception to "enrolment is always a manager decision" — for a family that already trains at the club, "the database is catching up to reality, not making a decision". The price follows automatically from the group's weekly volume through plan_for_volume, exactly as it does for a child, and the first prorated charge is raised in the same transaction.

04

Health — one declaration per child, and you always know which

Fourteen questions · three groups · one signature each

Today

One long scroll per child. The gate picks the first student who owes a declaration, renders the form, and reappears for the next one — so a family with three children meets it three times with no sense of being in a sequence at all.

The loop becomes visible. The header carries the child's name and their position, and a row of pills shows the whole queue at a glance: who is done, who is now, who is left. Nothing about the counter changes the five-step spine above it.

The healthy child, in one tap

The screen opens on a single question that answers all fourteen. Say yes and every question is marked לא and shown back as a list the parent can open and change — a declaration, not a skipped form. Say יש משהו and the three groups open, each keeping its own אין shortcut for the sections that are clear.

Not negotiable

The clause a parent confirms is derived from the answers and never chosen. clause.none and clause.limited are alternatives, not options — a family that reported asthma is shown the limited clause and cannot reach the other one. The signature sits under whichever clause the answers produced.

Each child's declaration carries its own signature. A parent signs three times, and that is the right trade: nothing about the third child's record then depends on a stroke drawn while looking at the first child's answers, and each declaration stays a self-contained document with its own clause and its own PDF.

05

Payment — four ways, and none of them a failure

Card · standing order · cash · cheques

Today

A plain list of choices per child, then a summary — and it is the last thing standing between a parent and the app they were invited to.

The club already knows which children are enrolled, what each costs and what the first charge is. This step asks one question — how would you like to pay? — once, and applies the answer to every child. The summary that follows is where a family that wants to split methods does so, one row at a time.

The three-child case

The common case — the same method for everyone — costs one tap instead of three. Splitting is still one tap per child, on a screen that already shows what each child costs.

Two of the four mean "pay in person"

Standing order, cash and cheques all end with the parent handing something over or arranging it with the manager. Each option carries a line saying what actually happens next, before it is picked — so nobody chooses cash and then discovers it means finding the coach with an envelope.

And all four end the same way: a confirmed arrangement naming the amount, the person and the moment. מסרו 660 ₪ למאמן באימון הראשון is a completed decision rendered with the same weight as a paid card — not a card payment that failed to happen.

Three families, one flow

The shape is decided per student, not per person. There is no adult flow and no parent flow — there is one flow that asks each question at the level the question belongs to. Everything below follows from a single query and a single test.

queue = SELECT student_id FROM guardian WHERE person_id = me
then, for each student in the queue: student.person_id == my person_id ? me : a child

For a family arriving on the link the queue is simply the student list they just wrote on step 3 — the checkbox puts the signer in it, each + הוספת ילד adds a child. For a family already on file it is the query above. Same queue either way, so steps 4 and 5 never need to know which kind of family they are serving.

A parent's name and ת.ז. is a fact recorded about a child, so the club knows who answers for a minor. Pickup permission is about collecting a child from training. student.grade is a school class. None of the three has a subject when the only student is the adult signing.

What the move actually costs

Those five columns already exist on person. Nothing new is added and no table is created — the change is which person row the registration service writes them to. save_registration currently does child_person.address = …; it should do signer_person.address = …, alongside the ת.ז. and immigration year it already writes there.

Nothing in the merge invents a field either. Every row above is collected today, by one form or the other; what changes is that they are collected on one screen, in one transaction, after consent rather than across two components on either side of a gate.

The argument is not mine. person.aliyah_year_encrypted already carries it, in writing: "On the parent, not the student, because that is whose fact it is: a two-child family would otherwise store it twice with nothing keeping the copies in agreement." Address, city and the three contact columns are the same fact about the same person, and they are stored twice today.

There is deliberately no household or family table, and this design does not ask for one. app/models/people.py says one "must never" be added, because after a separation there is no good answer to which household a child belongs to. "The household, asked once" is really "the signing person's own row, asked once" — which survives the separated-parent case intact, since each guardian carries their own address and each sees the flow from their own row.

What this surfaces

Six things found while reading the current implementation. The first two are structural; the third blocks a real user today.

  1. One family is typed into two forms that do not know each other. JoinFlow collects the parent's name and phone and every child's name, birthdate and groups. RegistrationStep then collects the address, city, phones, email, both parents and the pickup list — once per child. Thirteen of its fifteen fields are not about the child they are shown under. This is the mechanism behind "the form that repeats", and merging the two is the only fix that removes it rather than hiding it.
  2. Children are created before any consent exists. JoinShell renders the join form outside AuthedApp, so outside ConsentGate. Names, birthdates and phone numbers of minors are written first and the privacy policy is agreed to afterwards. The gate nesting in the same file argues the opposite for the health record, in so many words: consent asked after collection "has the consent doing no work".
  3. A self-enrolled adult is asked for their school class and their parents. selfStudent already ships. Tick it and the registration gate fires for you as a student — demanding grade, which is in REQUIRED_REGISTRATION_FIELDS and which an adult cannot answer, and rendering a card headed פרטי ההורים asking who your mother and father are. The gate is unpassable and the question is absurd. This is reachable today.
  4. Five household columns are stored once per child. Three children means three addresses with nothing keeping them in agreement, so a family that moves house updates one and stays wrong twice. person.aliyah_year_encrypted already carries the argument against this, in writing, for its own column.
  5. The two parent-name fields are one person. הורה נוסף · שם האם and שם האב write into a single other-parent record's first_name and last_name. A family cannot record both parents, and the labels promise something the storage does not do.
  6. The canvas promises an annual renewal that does not exist. The first-registration artboard reads ההצהרה תקפה לשנה. נזכיר לכם לחדש. while declaration.noExpiry says declarations never expire. Flagged in the 12j spec; repeated because this redesign touches that screen.

Moving the five columns

The address, city, home phone, mobile and email currently sitting on each child's row have to be seeded onto the guardian's. Most families agree with themselves and the copy is silent. The rule is for the ones that do not.

The rule
The most recently updated child's copy wins. Siblings disagree almost always because one record was updated and the other was not, so the newest copy is right nearly every time.
Nobody blocked
No family is sent back to step 3 and no column is left empty. A parent who did nothing wrong does not meet a gate because of a duplication we created.
Nothing silent
Every family whose copies disagreed lands on a short manager list — addresses to check — naming the family and which column differed. The manager is the only person who holds the knowledge to settle it.
Both parents
Each guardian is seeded from their own children's copies and keeps their own address afterwards. Separated parents at two addresses end up correct by construction — which is the case a household table could never have handled, and the reason there is not one.
One-time

After the migration, the child rows' copies of these five columns stop being written to and are the migration's business alone. Whether they are cleared or left in place as a fallback is a call for whoever writes it — but nothing should read them again.
