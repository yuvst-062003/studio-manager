How it fits together

The loop, end to end

Each stage feeds the next, and the last one feeds the first. Built separately these are four half-features; built as a loop, each one makes the others worth more.

The four parts

What each one actually does

Costs are per unit and verified against the providers’ own rate cards. Build times assume the pieces before them exist.

01 · AI content

~6 days ~2 shekels / month

The part that solves the real blocker. You don’t post because you don’t know what to post — not because writing takes too long. So this starts from something that actually happened and is already a row in the app.

  • A post from an event. A grading, a competition result, a new term — with the true date, the true age range and the true group name.
  • A caption from photographs. Pick three shots, get Hebrew copy and hashtags back.
  • A shot brief. Where to stand, what’s in frame, how long, and a script to read — so a coach with a phone gets a usable clip on the first take.
  • Hebrew subtitles from the clip’s own audio, at about 2 agorot a minute.

Everything lands in a box you edit. The model may phrase and interpret; it never decides a fact — prices, ages, times and addresses come from rows, always.

01b · The club’s own voice

~3 days $22 / month

You record about thirty minutes of yourself reading, once. After that the app can speak any script in your voice — so a Reel over Tuesday’s training needs no recording session, no quiet room, and no second take because a child shouted.

This removes the barrier that sits right behind the blank page. Writing the script was never the hard part; sitting down to record it at nine at night is.

  • The clone is one-time. Thirty minutes of clean audio, then it is a voice the app can use indefinitely.
  • About a minute of speech per clip, which is roughly 1,000 characters — well inside a $22 plan that carries about two hours a month.
  • It is your voice and only yours. Cloning a coach or a parent needs their written permission; the club should never hold a voice it was not given.

Hebrew is where this gets tested, not assumed. Hebrew text-to-speech is good enough to publish but still misplaces stress, and it struggles with exactly the thing Israeli speech does constantly: switching between Hebrew and English mid-sentence. A judo club adds a third layer — אוצ’י-גארי, איפון, רנדורי are Japanese words inside Hebrew sentences, which is the hardest case there is. Record one real script with the technique names in it and listen before committing to the plan.

Two rules that are not negotiable. It narrates content, never correspondence — nothing about a payment, a health declaration or a specific child is ever spoken by a machine wearing your voice. And when a clip is voiced rather than recorded, the club should be comfortable saying so if asked; parents know what you sound like, and a synthetic voice discovered is worse than one disclosed.

02 · WhatsApp approval

~6 days free to run

You ask for a post; the app drafts it and sends it to your WhatsApp; you reply “קצר יותר” and get a revision; you reply “אשר” and it goes out. Nothing is ever published without that yes.

Every message in that exchange is free, with no monthly cap, because you started the conversation — which is also why the design makes you the one who starts it. Ships over push notifications first (working today, no Meta account needed), then WhatsApp as a second transport.

03 · Facebook + Instagram

~8 days + App Review

Approved content posts itself to the Page and the Instagram Business account — feed posts, Reels and Stories — and Meta’s lead forms deliver enquiries straight into the app instead of sitting in Business Suite until somebody remembers to look.

  • Publishing is a two-step container upload per image or video. Tedious, well-documented, and it breaks on Meta’s schedule rather than yours — so it needs a red light when a token expires, not silent failure.
  • Lead capture needs five permissions and an App Review in which Meta’s reviewers test your live webhook. Days to weeks, and it can be rejected and resubmitted.
  • Meta keeps lead data for 90 days only, so an integration that stops quietly destroys leads rather than delaying them. That red light is not optional.

04 · The lead bot

~5 days free to run

One script, two places. The same four questions run as a chat on the landing page and as a WhatsApp conversation — written once, pointed at either transport, exactly as the approval loop is. A stranger gets a welcome, four questions, and a booked lesson. Their age picks the group, because group.age_min already says which; the slots come from the endpoint the landing page already uses; the booking is a real one.

Free to run: they message first, so the whole conversation sits inside the free window. Structured, not chatty — Meta bans open-ended assistants on WhatsApp from 15 January 2026 but explicitly permits booking and appointment bots, which is what this is.

What WhatsApp adds over the page is the follow-up, not the widget. A web visitor who abandons is gone and you have nothing. Someone who messaged you has already given you their number by the act of messaging, so a quiet lead can be nudged the next day for a few agorot. That is the entire difference, and it is the reason the bot’s real home is WhatsApp even though the page can run the same script.

The health declaration follows by link in both places — not because a chat cannot take a signature (a trial is declared, not signed) but because a health template is more questions than any chat should ask. The existing reminder worker chases it, and the child is not on the mat until it is done.

Why it is shaped like this

Q1

The child’s name — typed

No way around this one. It is also the only thing that makes every later message feel addressed to a person rather than a record.

Q2

Age — a list of five

This is why we ask age and not “which group”. A parent outside the club has no idea what קבוצה 2 is; the app does, from group.age_min. Five bands is too many for buttons — WhatsApp allows three — so it is a list.

Q3

The slot — real sessions

Rows come from the same trial-slots endpoint the landing page uses, so a full or cancelled lesson is never offered. Capped at ten by WhatsApp; the next three are enough.

Q4

The parent’s name — typed

Asked last, once they are already committed. The phone number is never asked — it is the number they are messaging from.

Three rules inside it

  • An escape hatch on the first message. לדבר עם מאמן hands the conversation to a human and stops the bot. A parent who wants a person and cannot find one is a parent who leaves.
  • It books, then says what is still missing. The booking is real the moment the slot is tapped — the declaration does not gate it, and the reminder worker chases it. But the message says plainly that the mat needs it first, because that is a safety rule and not paperwork.
  • It writes as it goes, never at the end. This is the one rule the on-page version must not break. The trial page was rebuilt on 8 September for exactly this: the booking used to be written last, so a parent who filled everything in and abandoned at the final step left the club nothing — no name, no number, no lead to ring. A chat is four steps, so it must create the lead the moment it has a name and a way to reach them, and enrich it afterwards.
  • It never quotes a price it was not given. מחירים ושעות answers from price_plan rows. A bot that invents 320 shekels to a prospective parent has done more damage than one that says nothing.

Worth knowing

The parts that can bite

None of these stops the plan. Each one has cost somebody a month elsewhere, so they are named here rather than discovered later.

Tokens expire quietly

A Meta page token that lapses stops publishing and stops lead delivery with no error on screen — and leads are gone for good after 90 days. Needs a red light tied to “last successful delivery”, not a hope.

App Review can be rejected

It is a person watching a screencast. Budget for one resubmission and do not promise a date to anybody before it clears.

Only consented faces

Publishing is gated on the photo consent shipped today. A clip with an unconsented child in frame must be blocked at the point of posting, not caught afterwards.

No broadcasts to parents

Marketing templates to 100 families is per-message, unignorable, and gets a number reported. Club announcements already reach an app the family chose to install.

The bot must stay structured

Meta bans open-ended AI assistants on WhatsApp from 15 January 2026. A booking flow is explicitly fine; a chatty general assistant risks the number.

Hebrew in a cloned voice

Stress patterns and Hebrew-English switching are where Hebrew TTS still slips, and Japanese technique names inside Hebrew sentences are the worst case of it. One test script with אוצ’י-גארי in it settles whether this is usable, and it costs nothing to find out.

Filming from inside the app

This is a PWA, and iOS camera access in an installed web app has a long history of failing. Prove it on a real iPhone before anyone estimates it — the shot brief works without it.
